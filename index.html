Your answer

If your cart changes, so can this answer. The contract permits late faculty adoptions, so materials can be added to a course after you make your decision — check your cart again near the deadline. And opting out is not a lockout: if a course later needs something, you can buy just that item, then.

What the Seawolf Bundle is

SSU’s version of Barnes & Noble’s First Day® Complete program. Every undergraduate is enrolled automatically and charged $21.00 per unit for course-material access — rental-first, so most physical items must be returned at term’s end — unless they opt out before the add/drop deadline.

The price is an average, not your cost

The per-unit price isn’t based on what your materials cost. The contract between Sonoma State Enterprises (SSE) and Barnes & Noble sets a single flat rate:

“…an average price across all courses” which applies “regardless of how many or whether course materials are being used in a particular course.” Amendment No. 1 §22(v)(1)

If your classes need less than the average, you pay the average anyway. That’s the gap this calculator closes.

Who profits, and who sets the price

SSE — the SSU auxiliary that holds the bookstore contract — receives a commission of 10% of gross course-material sales, a category the contract expressly defines to include bundle revenue §11.1 as amended. The per-unit price is set each year by agreement between SSE and Barnes & Noble, no later than March, effective the following fall §22(v)(5). The agreement contains no cap on price increases and excludes no programs §22(iii)(3): the student price has risen from $18.50 per unit in 2023 to $21.00 now.

The $67.15 story

In Fall 2026 the author’s own cart showed exactly one included item across 10 registered units: a paperback selling online for about $11 new (July 2026). The bundle price for 10 units is $210.00. The portal nonetheless displayed: “Your estimated savings on included materials is: $67.15.” Asked how that figure was calculated, the bookstore answered that savings are “calculated off publisher pricing, but there might be better deals out there” and suggested comparing options — adding, “Since you only need 1 book for 10 units, the Seawolf Bundle might not be the best option for you.”

This tool is that comparison — for every student, using your own cart as the source of truth. Sometimes the answer really is to stay in: single-use access codes usually can’t be bought used, and if your courses require a few of them, the bundle can win. The tool will tell you either way.

What this tool does and doesn’t do

It reads what your cart shows, has you confirm it, and does arithmetic. It never looks up prices behind your back and never asserts a price it didn’t source — the buy-it-yourself total is built from prices you find at the links it gives you. The full computation is documented in the methodology, and every factual claim on this page is sourced line-by-line in the claims ledger.
